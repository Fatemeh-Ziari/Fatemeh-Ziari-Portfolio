04 / RESEARCH

Why insured people rarely use their insurer’s app.

My M.Sc. thesis in Global Public Health studies how adults aged 20–40 in Germany engage with health insurance apps, what drives or limits that engagement, and what insurers can do about it.

M.SC. THESIS · 2026N = 98

The tool is there — but people are not using it.

More than 70 million people in Germany have statutory health insurance, almost every insurer offers an app, and since 2025 the app is usually the way into the electronic patient record (ePA). Availability is not the same as use: if people do not use these services, the expected benefits — less paperwork, better access to information, more efficient care — never arrive.

DESIGNOnline survey, German and English, March–April 2026
PARTICIPANTS98 adults aged 20–40, statutory (GKV) or private (PKV) insurance
FRAMEWORKTechnology Acceptance Model, extended with trust and privacy
APPROACHThree-path survey: users, aware non-users, unaware

KEY FINDINGS

Three gaps explain low engagement.

43.9%

Information gap

People do not know what the app can do — even active users. Lack of information was the top barrier; 18.4% knew none of the listed features, and 62.5% of non-users were unsure what the app offers.

81.3%

Motivation gap

The main value is saving time, but that only matters when there is a task, and insurance tasks happen a few times a year. 81.3% of aware non-users “never felt the need”; 31.6% of all respondents see no personal benefit.

65.3% vs 10.3%

Clinical utility gap

Users want a health tool, but the apps are used for paperwork. 65.3% want direct appointment booking, yet only 10.3% of users have booked through the app. Medical records (48.0%) and vaccination history (43.9%) are also in demand.

WHAT LIMITS REGULAR USE

The top barrier is information, not privacy.

All respondents, N = 98, up to 3 answers. Privacy ranks third: communication is cheaper and faster to fix than trust in data protection.

FOR INSURERS

Three levers, fixable without a huge new infrastructure.

01

Communicate features, not just the app

Explain specific features at the moment they become relevant: after a doctor visit, after a claim, at the start of the year — for example through contextual push notifications (wanted by 26.5%).

Low cost
02

Prioritise health features and easy login

Biometric login (wanted by 46.9%) is a design decision. Booking, records and vaccination history can build on the ePA infrastructure that already exists.

Low → medium cost
03

Make trust visible to non-users

Insurers already work under GDPR and strict German health-data law. Explaining this in plain language, with real user experiences, addresses the lower trust non-users expect.

No app change needed
What this study adds
Insurer apps studied as their own category, rarely done in research so far
Actual use measured (frequency and features), not only the intention to use
Users, aware non-users and unaware people analysed separately
The gap between wanted and used health features made concrete in numbers
